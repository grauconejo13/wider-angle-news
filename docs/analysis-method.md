# Analysis method

## Editorial promise

The Wider Angle should never describe its output as perfectly unbiased. Selection,
word choice, source access, and classification all involve judgment. The product
instead promises transparency, traceability, and visible uncertainty.

## Story-cluster structure

Every analyzed event should contain:

1. **Descriptive headline** — precise, non-sensational, and not copied from one source.
2. **Concise summary** — what occurred and why the event is being covered.
3. **Established points** — details supported by primary evidence or independent reporting.
4. **Still unclear** — missing information, disputed claims, and developing facts.
5. **Framing comparison** — what different reporting emphasizes, assumes, or omits.
6. **Angles** — relevant lenses beyond a simple political spectrum.
7. **Reader relationship** — Act, Influence, Prepare, Understand, or Observe.
8. **Sources** — links and roles for every material claim.

## Evidence labels

| Label | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Documented | Supported by an accessible primary record or direct evidence. |
| Corroborated | Independently reported by multiple credible sources. |
| Attributed | A statement made by a named person or organization. |
| Disputed | Credible accounts or interpretations conflict. |
| Developing | Material information is still unavailable or changing. |

Repeated publication does not automatically equal independent corroboration.
Several outlets may repeat the same wire report, press release, anonymous source,
or social-media claim.

## Source dimensions

Sources should be described through useful attributes rather than reduced to one
left-to-right label:

- primary record, original reporting, analysis, opinion, or advocacy;
- local, national, international, industry, community, or institutional;
- domestic view, neighboring-country view, or outside view;
- source ownership and funding when relevant;
- direct evidence and named-source quality;
- corrections history and factual reliability when supported by a transparent method.

## AI boundaries

AI may organize, classify, compare, and draft structured summaries. It must not:

- invent missing facts or citations;
- present majority repetition as proof;
- silently merge allegations with established information;
- infer a person's motive as fact;
- assign political or reliability labels without a documented method;
- remove source links from consequential statements.

Uncertain output should be flagged for review or omitted. The interface should
identify automated analysis and display when a story was last processed.
